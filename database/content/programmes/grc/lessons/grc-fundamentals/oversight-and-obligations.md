## Oversight needs information

A board cannot oversee what it cannot see. Oversight is the cycle in which leadership sets expectations, receives reliable information about performance against them, challenges it, and directs corrective action. The quality of that information decides the quality of oversight.

## Metrics that help leaders decide

| Type | What it measures | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Key performance indicator (KPI) | How well a process or control operates | 96% of critical patches applied within 14 days |
| Key risk indicator (KRI) | Whether exposure is rising towards appetite | Number of privileged accounts without MFA |
| Key control indicator | Whether a specific control is working | Access reviews completed on time this quarter |

Good metrics are tied to a decision or a risk, have a defined target and threshold, are measured the same way each period, and come from a reliable source. Counting things that are easy to count (emails blocked, training modules launched) rarely tells leaders whether risk is under control.

## Obligations: knowing what you must comply with

Compliance starts with an **obligations register**: a list of every requirement the organisation must meet, where it comes from, who owns it, and how compliance is demonstrated.

Sources of obligations include:

- **Laws and regulations**, such as data protection law (in Nigeria, the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023) and sector rules issued by financial, telecommunications or other regulators.
- **Contracts**, for example customer requirements to hold a certification or to notify them of breaches within a set time.
- **Industry schemes**, such as payment card security requirements for organisations that handle card data.
- **Voluntary commitments**, such as frameworks adopted by board decision or statements made on the company website.
- **Internal policies**, which bind staff once approved.

| Obligation | Source | Owner | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Notify the regulator of qualifying personal data breaches | NDPA 2023 | Data protection officer | Incident procedure; breach log |
| Notify Customer X of incidents within 24 hours | Master services agreement | Head of operations | Contract; incident communications |

## Compliance is a floor, not a ceiling

Meeting every obligation does not mean risk is acceptable; obligations are written for the average organisation, not your specific threats. Equally, a risk-based programme must still meet mandatory obligations even when the risk seems low. Mature GRC functions keep both views: the obligations register and the risk register, linked through shared controls.

## Reporting rhythm

A simple oversight rhythm for a smaller organisation: a monthly security report to executive management, a quarterly risk report to the board or its committee, and an annual review of policy, risk appetite and the security strategy.
