## Speaking the board's language

Executives decide where money and attention go. They need to know which risks exceed appetite, what it will cost to fix them, and what happens if they do not. They do not need control IDs or configuration details.

## Structure of an executive risk report

1. **Headline**: one or two sentences. *"Two of our top five risks exceed the board's appetite. Both can be brought within appetite in six months for an estimated ₦18m."*
2. **Risk position**: a heat map or short table of top risks with current and target ratings.
3. **Movement since last report**: what improved, what worsened and why.
4. **Decisions required**: explicit asks, with options and consequences.
5. **Remediation plan**: key initiatives, owners, milestones and cost.
6. **Appendix**: method, criteria and the full register for those who want detail.

## Writing well for executives

- Lead with the conclusion.
- Express impact in business terms: customers affected, money, regulatory exposure, operational downtime.
- Be honest about uncertainty: "We assess this as likely, based on weekly phishing attempts and the absence of MFA."
- Avoid fear, uncertainty and doubt. Credibility is your most valuable asset; overstated risks erode it.
- Keep the main report to two pages.
