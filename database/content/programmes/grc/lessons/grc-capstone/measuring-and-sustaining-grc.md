## After the report

A risk report that leads to no change is a cost, not a control. The final skill in GRC is turning an assessment into a programme that keeps working after the consultant, or the enthusiastic new CISO, has moved on.

## A GRC operating calendar

| Frequency | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly | Security metrics to management; overdue actions and exceptions reviewed |
| Quarterly | Top risks reassessed; board or committee risk report; access reviews; restore tests |
| Twice a year | Supplier reassessments for critical tier; policy exceptions re-approved or closed |
| Annually | Full risk assessment; policy reviews; CSF profile refresh; internal audit programme; management review; training refresh |
| On events | Reassess after major incidents, new systems, acquisitions, regulation changes |

Publish the calendar so owners know what is expected of them, and track completion as a metric.

## Measuring the programme

Combine three kinds of measure:

1. **Risk position**: number of risks above appetite; movement of top risks towards target ratings.
2. **Control health**: percentage of key controls tested and effective; MFA coverage; patch timeliness; backup restore success.
3. **Governance discipline**: actions closed on time; exceptions past expiry; repeat audit findings; policies past review date.

Trends matter more than single values. A steady fall in risks above appetite, quarter after quarter, is the clearest evidence that GRC is working.

## Tools

Many organisations start with spreadsheets and shared folders, and that is acceptable if they are controlled: one owner, version history, restricted editing, regular backups. Dedicated GRC platforms help when the number of risks, controls, suppliers and frameworks grows, mainly by linking them so that one change updates every view. A tool does not substitute for clear ownership and honest assessment.

## Culture

GRC teams succeed when the business sees them as helping it take risks safely, not as the department that says no. Practical habits:

- explain the *why* behind every requirement;
- offer options with costs, not single demands;
- record risk acceptances respectfully; the business is entitled to accept risk within its authority;
- celebrate closed actions and improved ratings, not only failures.

## Your professional development

Strong GRC practitioners combine framework knowledge with enough technical understanding to challenge evidence, and enough business understanding to speak in impact. Next steps might include deeper study of a specific framework, auditing practice, privacy law, or quantitative risk analysis. Remember that this programme's certificate shows course completion; recognised professional certifications are awarded by their own bodies.
