## From profiles to a plan

A gap analysis compares your **Current Profile** with your **Target Profile** and turns the differences into a prioritised, funded action plan. The CSF describes a repeating cycle: scope the profile, gather information, create the Current Profile, analyse gaps and create an action plan, implement it and update the profile.

## Step 1: Scope

Decide what the profile covers: the whole organisation, one business unit, or a single service such as a mobile banking app. A focused first profile that finishes is more valuable than an organisation-wide one that stalls.

## Step 2: Build the Current Profile honestly

For each selected outcome, record what is actually true today and the evidence for it. Use a simple scale, defined in advance, for example:

| Rating | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 0 Not achieved | Outcome not addressed |
| 1 Partially | Addressed informally or for part of scope |
| 2 Largely | Defined and mostly operating, with known gaps |
| 3 Fully | Operating across scope with evidence |

Rate from evidence, not intention. "We have a backup policy" is not evidence that backups can be restored.

## Step 3: Set the Target Profile

Targets come from risk, obligations and appetite, not from a desire to score 3 everywhere. A small firm with no development team may sensibly set a low target for secure software development outcomes and a high target for identity and backup outcomes.

## Step 4: Analyse gaps and prioritise

| Outcome | Current | Target | Gap | Linked risk | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PR.AA (privileged access) | 1 | 3 | 2 | R1 admin compromise (20) | High |
| RC.RP (recovery execution) | 0 | 2 | 2 | R2 failed restore (16) | High |
| PR.AT (awareness) | 2 | 3 | 1 | R1, R6 | Medium |
| DE.CM (monitoring) | 1 | 2 | 1 | R3 undetected intrusion (12) | Medium |

Prioritise by the risk reduction each gap closure delivers, then consider cost, effort, dependencies and obligations with deadlines. Quick wins with real risk reduction (enabling MFA that is already licensed) go first.

## Step 5: Action plan

Each action needs an owner, a due date, resources and a measurable completion criterion. Group actions into a roadmap of, say, 90-day, six-month and twelve-month horizons. Report progress against the roadmap, and re-profile at a fixed interval to show movement.

## Tiers in context

Tiers (1 Partial, 2 Risk Informed, 3 Repeatable, 4 Adaptive) characterise how cybersecurity risk governance and management are carried out: how formal, informed and adaptive the practices are. Use them to discuss the *rigour of practice*, alongside, not instead of, outcome-level gaps. Moving up a Tier should be a deliberate decision justified by risk, not a goal in itself.

## Common pitfalls

- Copying a target profile from another organisation without considering your own risks.
- Scoring generously to look good to the board, which hides the gaps the board needs to fund.
- Treating the profile as a one-off project rather than a living management tool.
