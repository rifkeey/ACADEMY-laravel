## How CSF 2.0 is organised

The CSF 2.0 **Core** is a hierarchy of outcomes:

- **Functions** (6): the highest-level grouping, such as Protect.
- **Categories** (22 in total): groups of related outcomes, such as *Identity Management, Authentication, and Access Control* (PR.AA).
- **Subcategories**: specific outcome statements, such as an outcome about managing identities and credentials for authorised users. Each has an identifier like PR.AA-01.

NIST publishes supporting resources online rather than inside the framework document: **Implementation Examples** (illustrative actions for each Subcategory), **Informative References** (mappings to other standards and guidance), **Quick-Start Guides** for specific audiences, and **Community Profiles** built by sectors.

## Categories at a glance

| Function | Categories (identifiers) |
| --- | --- |
| Govern | Organizational Context (GV.OC), Risk Management Strategy (GV.RM), Roles, Responsibilities, and Authorities (GV.RR), Policy (GV.PO), Oversight (GV.OV), Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management (GV.SC) |
| Identify | Asset Management (ID.AM), Risk Assessment (ID.RA), Improvement (ID.IM) |
| Protect | Identity Management, Authentication, and Access Control (PR.AA), Awareness and Training (PR.AT), Data Security (PR.DS), Platform Security (PR.PS), Technology Infrastructure Resilience (PR.IR) |
| Detect | Continuous Monitoring (DE.CM), Adverse Event Analysis (DE.AE) |
| Respond | Incident Management (RS.MA), Incident Analysis (RS.AN), Incident Response Reporting and Communication (RS.CO), Incident Mitigation (RS.MI) |
| Recover | Incident Recovery Plan Execution (RC.RP), Incident Recovery Communication (RC.CO) |

You do not need to memorise every identifier, but recognising the Category codes makes profiles and mappings much easier to read.

## Why Govern was added

CSF 1.1 had five Functions. Version 2.0 added **Govern** and placed it at the centre, because outcomes such as understanding the organisation's mission and stakeholders, setting risk appetite, assigning roles and authority, approving policy and overseeing performance shape every other Function. Supply chain risk management also moved into Govern, reflecting how much risk now arrives through suppliers.

A practical way to read Govern: if Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover describe *what the security programme does*, Govern describes *how leadership directs and checks it*.

## Using the Core well

1. **Outcomes, not instructions.** A Subcategory says what should be true, not which product to buy. Two organisations can meet the same outcome very differently.
2. **Not a checklist to complete.** Select the outcomes relevant to your risks and obligations; some may not apply.
3. **Functions run concurrently.** They are not sequential phases. Detect and Respond operate continuously, not "after" Protect is finished.
4. **Version your references.** When you cite a Subcategory, record that you used CSF 2.0; identifiers changed between versions, so mappings built for 1.1 need review.

## Who uses it

CSF 2.0 explicitly addresses all organisations, not only critical infrastructure. Its outcome language makes it a good common vocabulary between technical teams and boards, which is why many organisations report to leadership in CSF terms even when they are certified against another standard.
