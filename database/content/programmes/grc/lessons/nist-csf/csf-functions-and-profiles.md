## What CSF 2.0 is

The NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0, published in February 2024, is a voluntary, outcome-based framework for managing cybersecurity risk. It describes *what* good looks like rather than prescribing *how* to achieve it, which makes it usable by organisations of any size, sector or country.

## The six Functions

1. **Govern (GV)**: establish and monitor strategy, expectations and policy. New in 2.0.
2. **Identify (ID)**: understand assets, suppliers and risks.
3. **Protect (PR)**: safeguards such as identity management, training, data security and platform hardening.
4. **Detect (DE)**: find and analyse possible attacks.
5. **Respond (RS)**: act on detected incidents.
6. **Recover (RC)**: restore assets and operations.

Each Function contains **Categories** and **Subcategories**, which are specific outcome statements. NIST also publishes Implementation Examples and Informative References that link outcomes to other frameworks.

## Profiles

A **Current Profile** describes the outcomes you achieve today. A **Target Profile** describes the outcomes you need, given your risks, obligations and appetite. The difference is your gap, and the basis of an improvement plan.

```text
Outcome: PR.AA (Identity management, authentication and access control)
Current: MFA for email only; shared admin accounts on core banking
Target:  MFA for all remote and privileged access; named admin accounts
Priority: High (supports top-rated risk R1)
```

## Tiers

CSF Tiers (Partial, Risk Informed, Repeatable, Adaptive) describe how rigorous your risk management practices are. They are not a maturity score you must maximise. Choose the Tier appropriate to your risk.

## Prioritising

Rank gaps by the risks they address, not by how many Subcategories they close. Five well-chosen improvements tied to top risks are worth more than fifty cosmetic ones.
