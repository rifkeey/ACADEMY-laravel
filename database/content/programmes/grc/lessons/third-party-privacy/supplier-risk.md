## Your risk extends to your suppliers

Cloud providers, payroll processors, software vendors and outsourced IT all handle your data or connect to your systems. A breach at a supplier can expose your customers just as surely as a breach of your own network.

## A proportionate approach

1. **Inventory suppliers** and what data and access each has.
2. **Tier them** by criticality and sensitivity: a supplier hosting customer financial data needs far more scrutiny than a stationery vendor.
3. **Due diligence** before contract, proportionate to tier: security questionnaires, certifications (and their scope), independent assurance reports, penetration-test summaries, financial stability.
4. **Contract** for security: data protection obligations, breach notification timelines, right to audit, sub-processor controls, data return and deletion at exit.
5. **Monitor** throughout the relationship: periodic reassessment, incident notifications, certificate expiry.
6. **Exit** cleanly: confirm data deletion and access removal.

Always check certification **scope**. An ISO/IEC 27001 certificate covering a supplier's head office does not cover the data centre hosting your service.

## Data protection obligations

Privacy principles common to modern data protection laws include lawfulness, purpose limitation, data minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity and confidentiality, and accountability.

In Nigeria, the **Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023** established the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC) and sets obligations for data controllers and processors, including lawful bases for processing, data subject rights, security measures and breach notification. When a supplier processes personal data on your behalf, you remain accountable and must ensure appropriate contractual and technical safeguards. Check the NDPC's current guidance and regulations, as implementing rules continue to develop.
