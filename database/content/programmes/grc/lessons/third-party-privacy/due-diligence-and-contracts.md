## Tiering suppliers

Not every supplier deserves the same scrutiny. A tiering model scores each supplier on a few criteria and sets the depth of assessment accordingly.

| Criterion | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Data | Does the supplier store or process personal or confidential data? How much? |
| Access | Does it connect to our network or hold privileged credentials? |
| Criticality | Would its failure stop a critical service? How quickly could we replace it? |
| Regulatory relevance | Is the service subject to regulator rules on outsourcing? |

| Tier | Example | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Critical | Core banking host processing all customer data | Full questionnaire, evidence review, assurance reports, possibly on-site or remote audit; annual reassessment |
| High | Payroll processor with staff personal data | Questionnaire with evidence; certifications and their scope; reassess every one to two years |
| Medium | Marketing agency with limited contact data | Short questionnaire; contract clauses |
| Low | Stationery supplier | Standard terms only |

## Due diligence techniques

- **Security questionnaires**: useful for coverage, but self-reported. Ask for evidence on the answers that matter most.
- **Certifications**: check the certificate is current, issued by an accredited body, and that its **scope** includes the service and sites you will use.
- **Independent assurance reports**: for example, service organisation control reports prepared by independent auditors. A report that tests operating effectiveness over a period gives more assurance than one describing design at a point in time. Read the **exceptions** and the **complementary user entity controls**: the controls the supplier expects *you* to operate.
- **Penetration test summaries**: check date, scope, and whether high-severity issues were fixed.
- **Financial and operational stability**: a supplier going out of business is also a risk to your data.

## Fourth parties and concentration

Ask which **sub-processors** the supplier uses and where data will be stored. Your data may travel to a provider you never assessed, possibly in another country. Also consider **concentration risk**: if several critical suppliers depend on the same cloud region, one outage affects them all.

## Contract clauses that matter

| Clause | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Security requirements | Minimum controls, or compliance with a named standard, for the service in scope |
| Data protection terms | Processing only on documented instructions; confidentiality; assistance with data subject requests |
| Breach notification | Notify you without undue delay and within a fixed time (for example 24 hours), with defined content |
| Right to audit | You, or an independent auditor, may assess compliance; or the supplier provides assurance reports annually |
| Sub-processors | Prior notice or approval; the same obligations flow down |
| Data location and transfer | Where data may be stored; conditions for cross-border transfer |
| Termination and exit | Return of data in a usable format; certified deletion; transition assistance |
| Liability and insurance | Allocation of financial responsibility; cyber insurance levels |

A notification period longer than your own legal deadline is a red flag: if the law requires you to notify the regulator within 72 hours, a supplier who may tell you after 30 days makes compliance impossible.

## Deciding

The outcome of due diligence is a decision by the business owner: approve, approve with conditions (time-bound remediation and contract changes), or reject. Record residual risks in the risk register like any other risk.
