## The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 in outline

The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (NDPA) provides the legal framework for protecting personal data in Nigeria and established the **Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC)** as the regulator. The NDPC issues regulations and guidance under the Act; in 2025 it issued a General Application and Implementation Directive to explain how the Act applies in practice. Check the NDPC's site for the current instruments.

## Key roles

- **Data subject**: the individual the data is about.
- **Data controller**: decides the purposes and means of processing. A microfinance company is the controller for its customers' data.
- **Data processor**: processes data on the controller's behalf, such as a hosting provider or payroll bureau. The controller remains accountable for choosing processors that provide adequate safeguards.
- **Data protection officer (DPO)**: controllers must designate one with expert knowledge to advise on and monitor compliance.
- **Data controllers and processors of major importance**: a category defined by the Commission (for example by volume of data subjects or the nature of the data) that carries additional obligations, including registration with the NDPC.

## Principles and lawful bases

The Act sets out principles similar to other modern data protection laws: fair, lawful and transparent processing; specified purposes; data minimisation; accuracy; limited retention; appropriate security; and accountability. Processing needs a **lawful basis**: consent, performance of a contract, a legal obligation, vital interests, a task in the public interest or the exercise of official authority, or legitimate interests that are not overridden by the data subject's rights. Sensitive personal data, such as health or religious data, has stricter conditions.

## Obligations GRC teams usually own or support

| Obligation | What it involves in practice |
| --- | --- |
| Security of processing | Technical and organisational measures appropriate to the risk |
| Data protection impact assessment | Required before processing likely to result in high risk to individuals |
| Breach notification | Notify the Commission within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach likely to result in risk to individuals' rights; inform affected individuals promptly where the risk is high. Processors must notify the controller without delay. |
| Data subject rights | Processes to handle access, correction, deletion, objection and portability requests |
| Cross-border transfers | Transfer only where the destination offers adequate protection or another condition in the Act is met |
| Records and accountability | Be able to demonstrate compliance, including processor contracts |

Penalties under the Act can reach a percentage of annual gross revenue or a fixed sum, whichever is higher, with a higher ceiling for organisations of major importance. Reputational damage and customer loss often cost more.

## Compliance monitoring

Compliance is not a one-off project. A monitoring programme typically includes:

- an **obligations register** linking each requirement to owners and controls;
- a **compliance calendar**: annual filings or audits required by the regulator, policy reviews, DPIA reviews, supplier reassessments;
- **key indicators**: data subject requests answered on time, breaches notified within deadline, suppliers with signed data processing terms, staff training completion;
- **periodic testing**: sample-check that privacy notices, consent records and deletion processes work;
- **regulatory horizon scanning**: track new NDPC regulations, guidance and sector rules.

## Connecting privacy and security

Privacy risk and security risk overlap but differ. A system can be secure and still collect far more personal data than it needs, or use it for a purpose customers were never told about. Include privacy impacts in the risk register and involve the DPO in architecture reviews and supplier assessments.
