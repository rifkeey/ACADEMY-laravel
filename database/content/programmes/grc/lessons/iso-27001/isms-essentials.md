## What an ISMS is

An Information Security Management System (ISMS) is the set of policies, processes, roles and controls an organisation uses to manage information security risk systematically and improve it over time. ISO/IEC 27001 specifies requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining and continually improving an ISMS. The current edition is ISO/IEC 27001:2022.

> ISO standards are copyrighted and sold by ISO and national standards bodies. This lesson explains concepts in our own words; it does not reproduce the standard. Organisations implementing an ISMS should purchase the official text.

## Key concepts

- **Scope**: which parts of the organisation, locations, processes and systems the ISMS covers. A narrow scope is easier to certify but may mislead customers if they assume it covers everything.
- **Risk-based approach**: controls are selected because risk assessment shows they are needed, not because they appear on a list.
- **Annex A controls**: ISO/IEC 27001:2022 includes a reference set of 93 controls in four themes (organisational, people, physical, technological). ISO/IEC 27002 gives implementation guidance for them.
- **Statement of Applicability (SoA)**: a document listing each Annex A control, whether it is included or excluded, the justification, and its implementation status. Auditors use it heavily.
- **Internal audit and management review**: mandatory mechanisms for checking the ISMS works and for leadership to direct improvement.
- **Continual improvement**: nonconformities lead to corrective actions, which are tracked to closure.

## Certification and accreditation: say it accurately

- An organisation is **certified** by a certification body after an external audit against ISO/IEC 27001.
- Certification bodies are **accredited** by national accreditation bodies to show they are competent to certify.
- Individuals may hold auditor or implementer qualifications from personnel certification schemes.
- Completing an AMANIQ course gives you **knowledge**, not an ISO qualification. Never describe a course certificate as an ISO certification.
