## The management system loop

ISO/IEC 27001 is organised like other ISO management system standards. Its requirement clauses cover, in order: context of the organisation, leadership, planning, support, operation, performance evaluation, and improvement. Together they form a plan–do–check–act loop. This lesson focuses on the "check" and "act" parts, which are where many first-time ISMS implementations struggle.

## Internal audit

The organisation must audit its own ISMS at planned intervals to check that it conforms to its own requirements and to the standard, and that it is effectively implemented and maintained. Key principles:

- **An audit programme**, not a single audit: plan which processes and controls are audited when, giving more attention to important and changing areas.
- **Objectivity and impartiality**: auditors should not audit their own work. Small organisations often use a trained colleague from another department or an external consultant.
- **Defined criteria and scope** for each audit, and **reported results** to relevant management.

## Management review

Top management reviews the ISMS at planned intervals. Inputs typically include the status of previous actions, changes in context, audit results, nonconformities, monitoring results, feedback from interested parties, risk assessment results and improvement opportunities. Outputs are decisions: changes, resources and improvements. A management review that only "notes" a slide deck, with no decisions recorded, is weak evidence of leadership involvement.

## Nonconformity and corrective action

A **nonconformity** is a failure to meet a requirement. The organisation reacts to control the immediate problem (a **correction**), then finds the root cause and acts to stop it recurring (a **corrective action**), and checks that the action worked.

Example: an access review was skipped. *Correction*: perform the review now and remove stale accounts. *Corrective action*: the review depended on one person's calendar; assign a deputy and generate the review automatically from the HR system.

## The certification cycle

| Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Stage 1 audit | The certification body reviews documentation and readiness: scope, risk assessment, SoA, internal audit and management review. |
| Stage 2 audit | Assesses whether the ISMS is implemented and effective, through interviews, observation and evidence sampling. |
| Certification decision | Made by the certification body after nonconformities are addressed. Certificates normally run for three years. |
| Surveillance audits | Usually annually in years two and three, covering part of the ISMS each time. |
| Recertification | A fuller audit before the certificate expires. |

Auditors commonly grade nonconformities as **major** (a requirement is not met at all, or there is a systemic failure that casts doubt on the ISMS) or **minor** (an isolated lapse). Unresolved major nonconformities prevent certification.

## Reading a certificate critically

When a supplier shows you a certificate, check: the **certification body** and whether it is accredited (look for the accreditation body mark and verify it in the relevant directory), the **scope** wording, the **sites** listed, the **standard edition** (organisations certified to the 2013 edition had until October 2025 to transition to 2022), and the **expiry date**.
