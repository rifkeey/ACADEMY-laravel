## Risk appetite and tolerance

**Risk appetite** is the amount and type of risk the organisation is willing to pursue or retain to achieve its objectives. It is set by the board. **Risk tolerance** expresses appetite in measurable thresholds for specific areas.

| Appetite statement | Tolerance |
| --- | --- |
| "We have a low appetite for loss of customer data." | No risk to customer data may remain above a score of 9 for more than 90 days. |
| "We accept moderate risk to adopt new technology quickly." | Pilot systems may launch with documented medium risks if a remediation plan exists. |

Without appetite, risk assessments produce scores that nobody knows how to act on. With it, the scores lead to decisions: treat, escalate, or accept.

## The risk register

A risk register is the working record of identified risks and what is being done about them. Typical fields:

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| ID and title | Unique reference and short name |
| Scenario | Threat, vulnerability, asset and impact in one statement |
| Owner | The business person accountable for the risk |
| Inherent rating | Likelihood × impact before current controls |
| Existing controls | What already reduces the risk, with evidence |
| Residual rating | Rating after existing controls |
| Treatment decision | Treat, transfer, avoid or accept |
| Treatment plan | Actions, owners, due dates, cost |
| Target rating | Expected rating after the plan |
| Review date and status | When it will next be reassessed |

## Treatment plans

A treatment decision is not a treatment. "Treat" must be followed by specific actions: *"Enable MFA for all core banking administrators (IT manager, 30 November, ₦1.2m)"*. Each action should reduce likelihood, impact or both, and you should be able to explain how.

## Risk acceptance

Accepting a risk is legitimate when it is within appetite or when the cost of treatment exceeds the benefit. A proper acceptance records who accepted it, with what authority, on what reasoning, and until when. Acceptances expire; review them at least annually and whenever circumstances change.

## Keeping the register alive

- Review top risks at every management risk meeting, and the whole register at least annually.
- Reassess when something changes: a major incident, a new system, a new regulation, a merger.
- Link incidents and audit findings to risks; an incident that was "not on the register" reveals a gap in identification.
- Track trends: are residual ratings moving towards the targets?

## Quantitative approaches

Some organisations estimate risk in money, using ranges for event frequency and loss magnitude. Quantification helps compare security spending with expected loss reduction, but needs good data and honest uncertainty ranges. Many teams start with a qualitative matrix and quantify only their top risks.
