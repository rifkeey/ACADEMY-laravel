## Policies are living documents

A policy that nobody has reviewed in four years usually describes an organisation that no longer exists. Manage policies through a defined lifecycle:

1. **Identify need**: a risk, regulation, audit finding or business change.
2. **Draft**: with input from the people who must follow it.
3. **Review**: legal, HR, IT and business stakeholders check feasibility.
4. **Approve**: by the authority named in your governance framework.
5. **Publish and communicate**: make it findable; explain what changed and why.
6. **Implement**: standards, procedures and technical controls.
7. **Monitor**: measure compliance and exceptions.
8. **Review**: at a fixed interval (often annually) and after major changes.
9. **Retire**: when superseded, keeping archived versions.

## Version control

Each policy should show a version number, owner, approver, approval date, next review date and a change history. Keep previous versions: during an incident investigation or dispute, you may need to show what the policy said at the time.

## Exceptions

Sometimes a requirement cannot be met, for example a legacy system that cannot support MFA. A formal exception process records:

- the requirement not met and why;
- the resulting risk and any compensating controls;
- who approved the exception (at a level matching the risk);
- an **expiry date** and remediation plan.

Unmanaged exceptions become permanent gaps. Review the exception register regularly.

## Traceability

Good governance can trace a line from each policy statement to the standards that implement it, the controls that enforce it, and the evidence that proves it. Traceability makes audits faster and reveals policy statements that nothing actually implements.
